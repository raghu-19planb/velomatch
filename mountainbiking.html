Let's find your mountain bike.

None of this requires expert terminology going in — we explain the jargon as we go.

Your riding

Suspension

A hardtail (front suspension only) is lighter and more efficient pedaling; full suspension soaks up rough terrain but costs more and weighs more.

Drops your saddle at the touch of a lever for descents, then raises it back for pedaling — standard on most trail/enduro bikes now, less common on XC race bikes.

Your fit

Mountain bikes are sized by letter (XS-XL), not a cm number the way road bikes are — so that's what we ask for here.

172 cm

The bike itself

A step-through frame has a low or missing top tube — easier to swing a leg over, traditionally sold as a "women's" bike. A step-over frame is the traditional diamond shape, traditionally sold as a "men's" bike. Either shape genuinely works for any rider. Real mountain bikes also need diamond-frame rigidity for off-road loads — this catalog doesn't have step-through mountain bikes, so this question mostly serves as an honest heads-up rather than a filter.

Fig. 007 — where this is headed

To everyone who's stood in a bike aisle guessing,

Buying a bike usually comes down to a guess: a size sticker on a rack, a salesperson pointing at whatever's in stock, or a color you liked in a catalog. Get it wrong and it's money spent on something that never quite fits.

We think fit should come from geometry, not guesswork — reach, stack, standover, all measured and matched to the rider, whoever that rider is.

Rideometry is built to do that matching for every kind of rider and every kind of ride. Right now it's five working prototypes: a first bike for a growing kid, a daily commuter, a bikepacking rig, a mountain bike, and a racing or ultra-endurance bike — because those are the cases where a wrong guess costs the most. The same approach — real measurements, scored against real bikes — is what we're building toward next, for every rider after this one.

It won't get everything right yet. But it will show its work — the frame size, the suspension travel, the gear range — so you're choosing a bike, not gambling on one.

This is where we're starting. Not where we're stopping.
