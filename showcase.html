How this page works

A bike is tagged New only if its maker says so: a “New” label, an all-new range, or a 2027 model-year page. The rest are bikes on sale now from the brand or a Canadian shop. Every bike has a live product page and a photo we checked.

Prices are shown only when they are in Canadian dollars, as the “from” price on the day we checked, so they can change before you buy. Where a brand’s page doesn’t show a Canadian price, we say so instead of guessing.

Photos load from each brand’s or shop’s own website and belong to them. We link to them and don’t pass them off as ours. If a photo goes missing, the bike still links to its page.

We re-check every link and photo each quarter and add the new arrivals.

    Fig. 005 — where this is headed

    To everyone who's stood in a bike aisle guessing,

    Buying a bike usually comes down to a guess: a size sticker on a rack, a salesperson pointing at whatever's in stock, or a color you liked in a catalog. Get it wrong and it's money spent on something that never quite fits.

    We think fit should come from geometry, not guesswork — reach, stack, standover, all measured and matched to the rider, whoever that rider is.

    BikeSpoke is built to do that matching for every kind of rider and every kind of ride. Right now it's five working prototypes: a first bike for a growing kid, a daily commuter, a bikepacking rig, a mountain bike, and a racing or ultra-endurance bike — because those are the cases where a wrong guess costs the most. The same approach — real measurements, scored against real bikes — is what we're building toward next, for every rider after this one.

    It won't get everything right yet. But it will show its work — the frame size, the gear capacity, the climbing range — so you're choosing a bike, not gambling on one.

    This is where we're starting. Not where we're stopping.
